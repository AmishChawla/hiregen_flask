Capstone: run one requisition end to end

Module 12, Lesson 4  ·  3 min read ·  Updated 21 September 2026

Module 12 · Lesson 4

In short

  • Run one real role from start to finish using everything you built.
  • Write down how it went. Writing it is the point.
  • Change no more than two things before the next one.

Run one real job from start to finish using everything you have built. This is where it either holds together or shows you what is missing.

What to do

Pick a job you are genuinely hiring for. If you have none, use the practice job and a colleague as the hiring manager.

  1. Intake and blueprint. The twelve questions, the profile with its gaps marked, the scorecard, three must-haves, weighted scores.
  2. Sourcing. Widen your search terms, build the search, produce a longlist with the source recorded for each person.
  3. Screening. Check how well the CVs were read, enter your rules, run it, do the disagreement check, record the rules with the shortlist.
  4. Ranking. Build the match profile from your scorecard, rank, and write one justification per candidate quoting their CV.
  5. Outreach. Five-message sequence for anyone you approached, checked before sending.
  6. Interviews. Structured loop, written score descriptions, scores locked before the debrief.
  7. Decision. Debrief against the bar, recorded with evidence.
  8. Records. Rules saved, rejections given with reasons, every AI step covered by your notice.

What to hand in

A one-page write-up of your own run: what you did at each step, what took longer than expected, where the process broke, and the one change you would make first.

Writing it is the exercise. A run you do not write up teaches you much less, because the things that went slightly wrong get forgotten within a week.

Where these runs usually break

StepCommon problemUsually means
IntakeThe manager will not trade anythingGo back to question 12. Do not start searching
SourcingThe pool is far smaller than expectedA requirements conversation, not a search problem
ScreeningEveryone scores about the sameYour rules are not separating people
OutreachSilenceTargeting, not writing. Check who you contacted
InterviewsInterviewers score after discussingLock the scores. Nothing else fixes this
Decision"Culture fit" appearsAsk which skill. Every time
RecordsYou cannot reconstruct why someone was rejectedSave the rules with the shortlist from now on

Judge your own run

  • Could someone else repeat this using only what you wrote down?
  • Could you explain any rejection to the person it affected, in plain words?
  • Did the disagreement check change anything? If not, look at why.
  • Which step took longest, and was it the step that mattered most?
  • What would you do differently — and is it a rule change or a process change?

One job, then improve

Do not run five at once to gather data faster. One run, written up, changed, then the next. Teams who scale before the first write-up scale their mistakes along with everything else.
