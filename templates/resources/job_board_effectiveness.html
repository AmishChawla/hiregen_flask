How the formulas work

Job board effectiveness comes down to two numbers looked at together — what each hire cost, and what share of applicants actually converted.

Job board effectiveness formulas Cost per Hire = Spend ÷ Hires Hire Conversion % = (Hires ÷ Applications) × 100

Looking at either metric alone can mislead you. A source with a low cost per hire might simply be producing very few hires from a huge, low-quality applicant pool. A source with a high conversion rate might still be expensive per hire if application volume is low. Comparing both together separates genuinely efficient sources from ones that only look good on one dimension.

Example: $500 spent on a niche board that produces 3 hires from 60 applications works out to a $167 cost per hire and 5% conversion. $2,000 spent on a broad job board producing 4 hires from 400 applications works out to a $500 cost per hire and 1% conversion — the niche board is the better source even though it produced fewer total hires.

How to choose the best job boards

  1. Rank by cost per hire first, conversion second. Cost per hire tells Finance what they need to know; conversion tells you why.
  2. Reallocate, don't just add. Shift budget away from underperforming sources rather than layering on new ones.
  3. Segment by role type. A board that performs well for entry-level roles may perform poorly for specialized or senior roles — compare within role categories, not across them.
  4. Review quarterly. Board performance shifts with seasonality, pricing changes, and traffic quality, so a one-time comparison goes stale.
  5. Don't ignore quality of hire. The cheapest source per hire isn't a win if those hires underperform or leave early — pair this data with retention and performance signals where you can.

Hire Conversion % = (Hires ÷ Applications) × 100 — the percentage of applicants from a source who ultimately became hires.

General high-traffic boards commonly convert well under 1% of applicants; niche boards and employee referrals often convert several times higher because the applicant pool is more pre-qualified.
