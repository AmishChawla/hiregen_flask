Scorecards and competency models

Module 2, Lesson 4  ·  4 min read ·  Updated 21 September 2026

Module 2 · Lesson 4

In short

  • A scorecard is a plan for what proof you will collect, not a list of skills.
  • Write each item so two people watching the same interview would agree on the score.
  • Delete 'cultural fit'. It measures nothing and hides bias.

A scorecard is not a list of skills. It is a plan for what proof you will collect, from where, and what counts as good enough — written down before you meet anyone.

Why bother

Two reasons. It makes two interviewers mean the same thing when they say someone was "strong". And it gives you a record of why a decision was made.

Both matter more once AI is involved, because the scorecard is where your screening rules come from in Module 4. A vague scorecard gives you vague rules, which give you a shortlist you cannot explain.

Write things you could actually watch someone do

The test for any line: could two people watching the same interview agree, on their own, whether it happened?

Not thisThis
Strong communicatorExplains a technical trade-off to a non-technical colleague without losing the detail
Team playerDescribes a disagreement with a colleague and what they changed afterwards
Detail orientedSpots the error in the sample data and says what they would check next
Self-starterHas built something nobody asked for, and can explain why they chose it
Cultural fitDelete this line. It measures nothing and it is where bias hides

The right-hand column is longer, and that is the point. If you can describe something in two words, you have not defined it.

Decide what each score means, in advance

Use four levels, not five. An odd number collects a pile of middles. And write a description for each level that is specific to this job, not a generic word.

ScoreMeans
1 — No proofDid not show it, or showed the opposite
2 — PartlySome proof, but smaller scale or with help
3 — YesClear proof at the level this job needs
4 — BeyondClear proof beyond what this job needs

Write down what a 3 looks like for each item before the first interview. That single sentence does more for consistency than any amount of interviewer training. It is also the sentence you will reuse as a screening rule later.

Every item needs a place to be checked

Decide where each thing will be assessed, and be honest about what each stage can actually show.

StageCan showCannot show
Reading the CVScale, industry, tools, what they have shippedAnything about how they behave
InterviewReasoning, past behaviour, judgement under questioningWhether the work stays good over months
Work sampleWhether they can actually do the taskHow they work with others over time
ReferencePatterns over time, working styleAnything the referee did not see

The rule: if nothing in your process can check it, cut it. It is not a requirement then. It is a preference, and it will get applied inconsistently and invisibly.

How long should it be

Five to seven items. Beyond that, interviewers stop scoring and start writing summaries, and the scorecard becomes decoration. If your list is longer, most of the extra items are either learnable on the job or impossible to measure — both good reasons to remove them.
